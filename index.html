A Journey Through Indian History

From the rise of the Mughals to the fall of the last Emperor

The 1500s — Birth of an Empire

1526: A young conqueror named Babur crosses into India and crushes the Delhi Sultanate at Panipat — the Mughal Empire is born.

The empire nearly collapses when Humayun is exiled, only to claw its way back to power.

By century's end, Akbar the Great sits on the throne, weaving together a vast, diverse empire through conquest, marriage alliances, and religious tolerance.

The 1600s — The Golden Age

Jahangir rules over a court famous for its art, gardens, and Persian-influenced culture.

Shah Jahan takes the throne and pours the empire's wealth into marble and memory — the Taj Mahal rises as a monument to love and loss.

By the century's end, Aurangzeb pushes Mughal borders to their furthest reach, but cracks are already forming beneath the surface.

The 1700s — Cracks in the Crown

Aurangzeb's death in 1707 leaves a power vacuum; the empire that once seemed eternal starts to fracture.

1739: Nadir Shah storms in from Persia, sacks Delhi, and drags away the legendary Peacock Throne — a humiliating blow.

While Mughal power fades, new players enter the stage — Marathas, Sikhs, and European trading companies quietly building influence along the coasts.

1757: The British East India Company, through cunning and cannon fire, wins the Battle of Plassey — a single battle that changes India's destiny.

The 1800s — Sunset of the Mughals

The Company tightens its grip decade by decade, swallowing kingdoms through wars, treaties, and the controversial Doctrine of Lapse.

1837: Bahadur Shah Zafar becomes Emperor — a poet-king ruling in name only, a shadow of the empire his ancestors built.

1857: Fury boils over. Sepoys rise in revolt, and the aging Zafar is thrust forward as the reluctant face of India's first great uprising against colonial rule.

1858: The rebellion is crushed. Zafar is captured, tried in his own Red Fort, and exiled across the sea to Rangoon.

1862: In a foreign land, far from Delhi, the last Mughal Emperor dies — closing a 330-year dynasty and opening the era of the British Raj.
